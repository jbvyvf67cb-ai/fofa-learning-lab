## Lesson 1 — From ions to compounds *(migrated from the old atomic-structure doc)*
**Goal:** connect charge (from M1) to why atoms stick together.

Atoms are "happiest" with a **full outer shell** (like the unreactive noble gases). To get there:
- **Metals** tend to **lose** electrons → **positive** ions.
- **Nonmetals** tend to **gain** electrons → **negative** ions.

Then **opposite charges attract**, so the ions stick together into a **compound**. Example:
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → **NaCl** (table salt).

**Key words:** valence electrons, compound, chemical bond.
**Try it:** Pick a metal and a nonmetal; watch electrons transfer and the ions form.

**Check yourself:**
1. Do metals usually gain or lose electrons? _A: lose (become positive)._
2. Why do a + ion and a − ion stick together? _A: opposite charges attract._
3. What compound forms from Na⁺ and Cl⁻? _A: NaCl._
