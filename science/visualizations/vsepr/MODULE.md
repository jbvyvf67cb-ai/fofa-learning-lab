# M6 · Molecular Shape (VSEPR) — module content

**Explore:** the VSEPR Molecular Geometry viz (this folder).
**Status:** ✅ written (mirrors `module.js`, which the shell renders).
**Owns:** electron domains → 3D shape; how shape decides **molecular polarity**.
Builds on M5 (covalent bonds). Leans high-school.

## Planned lesson cards
1. **Electron pairs repel.** Groups of electrons around a central atom (bonds *and* lone pairs)
   push as far apart as possible — that spacing sets the shape.
2. **Naming the shape.** Count bonding groups and lone pairs (AXE): linear, bent, trigonal
   planar, pyramidal, tetrahedral… The name counts only the atoms you can see.
3. **Lone pairs bend things.** Why water is bent, not straight.
4. **Shape decides polarity.** Even with polar bonds, a symmetric shape can cancel out → the
   whole molecule is nonpolar (CO₂). An unsymmetric one is polar (H₂O).

## Try-it prompts
- Set bonds + lone pairs; rotate the 3D shape.
- Compare CO₂ (linear, nonpolar) with H₂O (bent, polar).
- Find a shape where polar bonds cancel to nonpolar (e.g., CH₄, SF₆).

## Quiz seeds
- (mc) What pushes electron groups apart? _A: they repel each other (like charges)._
- (mc) CO₂ is linear and → **nonpolar** / polar.
- (mc) Water is bent because of its → extra protons / **lone pairs** / neutrons.
- (interactive) Build a molecule that is symmetric and therefore nonpolar.
