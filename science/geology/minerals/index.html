Minerals

Rocks are made of minerals. Become a mineral detective: scratch, streak, fizz and stick your way to the answer.

1 What's a mineral?

A mineral is a natural, solid, non-living substance with its own exact recipe of atoms, lined up in a repeating pattern called a crystal. Salt is a mineral. So is the quartz in sand. A rock is a mix of minerals: granite is speckled because it's made of quartz, feldspar and mica grains all stuck together.

There are thousands of minerals, so geologists identify them with simple tests. The trick is that color can fool you. The same mineral can come in many colors, and different minerals can look alike.

2 Learn the tools

Hardness is how hard a mineral is to scratch. Geologists rank it from 1 (softest) to 10 (hardest) on the Mohs scale. A harder thing scratches a softer one, never the other way around. You can test it with things you own:

Streak is the color of a mineral's powder when you rub it on a rough white tile. Streak doesn't change the way the outside color can, so it's a great clue. Luster is how it shines: metallic (like metal), glassy, pearly, or dull. And some minerals have a special trick: one sticks to a magnet, one fizzes in vinegar.
