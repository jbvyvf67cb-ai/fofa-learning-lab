A Probability → Statistics course, built from counting.

Every idea here you can roll, flip, or count until it clicks. You won't memorize a probability formula — you'll build the sample space, count the outcomes that work, and watch real dice drift onto the exact numbers. We start with the chance of a single die face and end up describing real data. Only arithmetic and a little algebra are used anywhere.

Unit A · Probability with dice — the fundamentals
Lesson 1

The Single Die

What is probability? We draw the six-face sample space, roll it, and learn to write a chance as a fraction, decimal, and percent — using nothing but counting.

Ready Lesson 2

Two Dice & the Sample Space

Add a second die and draw every outcome as a 6×6 = 36 grid you can count. Roll it and see that all 36 squares are equally likely.

Ready Lesson 3

Sums & Permutations

Mark the squares that add to a total, see why (1,5) and (5,1) both count, and watch the “bell” shape appear straight out of counting the flat 36-grid.

Ready Lesson 4

Coins & “At Least One”

The flip-it-around complement rule (P(not A) = 1 − P(A)), a sample space that doubles into a tree (2N), and the “at least one” shortcut — count the one way to fail and subtract.

Ready Lesson 5

Combinations

When order doesn’t matter: group coin flips by how many Heads, meet “n choose k”, and build Pascal’s triangle by counting — every number the sum of the two above it.

Ready Lesson 6

Conditional Probability & Trees

When one event changes the next: draw marbles without replacement, build a probability tree, and multiply along the branches / add across them.

Ready Lesson 7

Monty Hall — should you switch?

The famous 3-door puzzle. Count the tiny sample space, play it, then simulate hundreds of games and watch “switch” win 2/3 of the time — proof that counting beats gut feeling.
