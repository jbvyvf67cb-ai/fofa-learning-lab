Monty Hall — should you switch?

A game show puzzle that fools almost everyone. Our tool — counting the sample space — settles it cold.

Three doors. Behind one is a car 🚗; behind the other two, goats 🐐. You pick a door. The host — who knows where the car is — opens a different door to reveal a goat, then asks: do you want to stay with your door, or switch to the last one? Most people shrug and say "50/50, doesn't matter." Let's count and see.

1 Count the whole sample space

There are only three equally-likely cases — where the car actually is. Say you always pick Door 1 (it doesn't matter which; the doors are the same by symmetry). Here's what happens in each case:

Counting beats intuition: switching wins in 2 of the 3 cases. The reason is the host's help — see Step 3.

2 Play a few rounds

Pick a door. The host opens a goat. Then choose to stay or switch, and see what you win.

3 Let the numbers decide

A few games are noisy. Play hundreds and the truth appears: the switch win-rate marches to 2/3, stay to 1/3 (the dashed marks).

Why? Your first pick is right only 1/3 of the time — so 2/3 of the time the car is behind one of the other two doors. The host then opens the goat among those two, squeezing that whole 2/3 onto the single door you could switch to. Switching harvests it.
