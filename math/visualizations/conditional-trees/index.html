Conditional Probability & Trees

Coins and dice never remember. But a bag of marbles does — draw one and the next chances change. A tree keeps track.

Every chance so far came from things with no memory: a die doesn't care what you rolled last. But pull a marble from a bag and don't put it back — now the bag has changed, so the next draw's chances change too. That "it depends on what already happened" is conditional probability.

Same bag, two different next-chances — it depends on the first draw. (Put the marble back instead — “with replacement” — and the second draw is unchanged: that's the independent case from the dice and coins.)

2 The probability tree

Draw two marbles (keeping the first). A tree lays out every path. The number on each branch is its chance, and to get a whole outcome you multiply along the path. Click a leaf.

The four leaf-chances always add up to 1 — something must happen.
