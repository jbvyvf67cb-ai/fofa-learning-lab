Spread

Two teams can have the same average score and play nothing alike. Spread measures how bunched-up or scattered the data is.

1 Range — the simplest spread

The range is just the biggest minus the smallest. Quick to find, but it only looks at the two end values — one far-flung dot blows it up.

range = largest value − smallest value

2 Typical distance from the middle

A fairer measure asks: on average, how far is each dot from the mean? Those are the teal sticks — average their lengths and you get the "typical distance from the middle." Bunched data → short sticks → small spread. Scattered data → long sticks → big spread. (Grown-ups square these distances to get the famous standard deviation, but the idea is the same: average distance from the center.)

3 The box plot — where the middle half sits

A box plot is a tidy picture of spread. The line stretches from the smallest to the largest. The box holds the middle half of the data (from the quarter mark to the three-quarter mark), and the green line inside is the median. A short box means the middle of the data is tightly packed.
