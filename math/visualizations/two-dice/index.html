Two Dice & the Sample Space

One die had six outcomes. Let's add a second die and draw every outcome — then count them.

In Lesson 1, the sample space for one die was the six faces. Now we roll two dice — call them die A and die B. The big question of this lesson: how many things can happen now, and can we still draw them all? (Spoiler: yes.)

1 Draw every outcome

Die A can land 6 ways. And for each of those, die B can also land 6 ways. So we make a grid: rows are die A, columns are die B. Every square is one possible roll — die A showing one number, die B showing another.

6 rows × 6 columns = 36 squares. That's the whole sample space for two dice — bigger than one die, but still something you can draw and count.

2 Every square is equally likely

Just like the six faces of one die, all 36 squares are equally likely. A roll of two dice is simply landing on one square. Roll a bunch and watch the squares fill in evenly — no square is special.

Or click any square to pick one exact roll and see its chance.

Why exactly 36? Because for each of die A's 6 outcomes, die B adds 6 more. 6 groups of 6 = 6 × 6 = 36. That multiplying is all "chaining dice together" means — and it drops right out of the picture. Each single square is 1 out of 36.
