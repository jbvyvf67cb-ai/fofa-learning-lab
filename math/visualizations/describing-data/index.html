Describing Data

Probability was about what might happen. Statistics is about numbers we already have — and how to sum them up in one “typical” value.

Say seven friends each scored some points in a game. That's a data set — just a pile of numbers. Instead of listing all seven, we often want one number that says "this is about typical." There are three famous ones. Drag the dots and watch all three react.

Drag any dot along the line. Dots that land on the same number stack up.

1 Mean — the balance point

The mean (the "average") is what everyone would get if you shared the total equally: add up all the numbers and divide by how many there are. It's also the balance point — the orange ▲ is exactly where the line would balance like a see-saw. Drag a dot far right and watch the balance point slide to follow it.

mean = (add up all the values) ÷ (how many values)

2 Median — the one in the middle

Put the numbers in order and the median (green line) is the one right in the middle — as many above it as below. With 7 dots it's the 4th one. (With an even count, it's halfway between the middle two.)

3 Mode — the most common

The mode is simply the value that appears most often — the tallest stack of dots. A data set can have no mode (all different) or several.

4 Why have three? Watch an outlier

Press “Make one an outlier” — it flings one dot way out to the edge. Notice the mean lurches toward it, but the median barely moves. One weird value (a billionaire walking into a room) can drag the average around, while the middle stays honest. That's why we keep more than one.
