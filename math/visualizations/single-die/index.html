The Single Die

What is probability? We'll figure it out with one ordinary die — one step at a time.

A die is just a cube with the numbers 1 to 6 on its faces. When you roll it, one face lands up. That's it. From this one little object we can learn what probability really means — no formulas you can't see a reason for. Let's go slowly.

1 What can happen?

Before we talk about chances, let's just list everything that could happen when you roll one die. Here they are — all of them:

These six pictures are everything that can happen. Mathematicians call the whole list the sample space — a fancy name for "all the things that could happen." Roll the die: whatever you get is always one of these six. Nothing else is possible.

2 A fair die has no favourites

A fair die doesn't prefer any number. Each of the six faces is equally likely — it has the same chance as every other. Hard to believe? Roll a bunch and watch. A few rolls look lumpy and random. But keep going…

The more you roll, the more the six bars even out. That's what "equally likely" looks like: given enough rolls, every face shows up about the same amount.

3 So what is a probability?

Probability is just counting. It's how many faces make your wish come true, divided by how many faces there are in total:

probability = (faces that count) ÷ (all six faces)

Rolling a 4? Only one face is a 4, out of six faces → 1 ÷ 6. Click the faces below to choose which ones "count" as a win, and watch the chance change. Try the shortcut buttons too.

Quick picks:
Pick some faces to see the chance.

See how the answer is always (green faces) out of 6? A probability is never bigger than 1 (all six faces — certain) and never smaller than 0 (no faces — impossible).

4 More sides, smaller chance

Dice come in other sizes: 8 sides, 20 sides, more. The rule doesn't change — one number out of N faces is 1 ÷ N. But notice: the more faces there are, the rarer any single number becomes, because the sample space is bigger.

Every face is still 1 out of N — the whole sample space is drawn above, and exactly one square in it is your number.
