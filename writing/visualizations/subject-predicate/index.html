Subject & Predicate

Every sentence has two jobs: to name something, and to tell what it does or is. Those two halves are the subject and the predicate.

The two halves of a sentence

Split almost any sentence down the middle and you get a subject and a predicate. Here's the trick you already know from Unit 1: the heart of the subject is a noun or pronoun, and the heart of the predicate is a verb.

SubjectWho or what the sentence is about. Its core is a noun or pronoun.
PredicateWhat the subject does or is. Its core is the verb.

How to find them

  1. Find the verb first.The action or being word is the heart of the predicate — e.g. flew, smiled.
  2. Ask “who or what?” before the verb.Who flew? The child. That noun (or pronoun) is the heart of the subject.
  3. Everything grouped with each core joins that half.The curious child | flew a bright kite. Two halves, one complete thought.
