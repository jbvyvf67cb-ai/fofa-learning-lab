Two complete thoughts are sitting side by side. Pick a connector to fuse them into one sentence — a coordinating word treats the two clauses as equals, a subordinating word makes one clause depend on the other. The same pair of ideas can combine several valid ways, each with a slightly different feel.

Coordinating Joins two equal, independent clauses (often with a comma) into a compound sentence. Either clause could stand alone.
Subordinating Turns one clause into a dependent setup for the other, forming a complex sentence. The dependent clause can't stand alone.
